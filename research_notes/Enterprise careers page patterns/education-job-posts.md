# Education and creator instructor job posts (October 2026)

Research date: 13 October 2026. Notes paraphrase public pages that were opened. Search-index text is labeled when a live fetch did not return the posting. No cybersecurity how-to is included; the Udacity mentor ad is described only as a support role.

## Which postings were actually open, and what was the closest role?

### Inferences
- A small channel copying “how education companies hire instructors” should copy the still-open content roles (Khan, DataCamp’s instructor path and curriculum/trainer ads, Udacity’s contractor mentor and maintenance ads), not assume Coursera or MasterClass currently post a teacher seat.
- Google’s live “YouTube” learning job is internal sales training. It is a poor template for a public YouTube instructor and a good template for how a large company specifies artifacts, audience, and pay without writing the syllabus.

## What headings appear, in order?

### Takeaway
Live ads use a short stack: who the company is, the role in one block, a responsibility list, a qualification list, then pay and legal caveats. They rarely use a heading that names the syllabus, the review calendar, or the interview loop.

### Cited Findings
- Khan Academy, both opened roles, uses the same order: scam notice; ABOUT KHAN ACADEMY; OUR COMMUNITY; THE ROLE; then either “Responsibilities” (Kids) or “What You’ll Do” (ELA); WHAT YOU BRING; PERKS AND BENEFITS; a pay-band paragraph; MORE ABOUT US; OUR COMPANY VALUES (seven named values); an equal-employment paragraph. The Kids role inserts ABOUT KHAN ACADEMY KIDS after the company blurb. The application form, after the ad, asks role-specific questions that are not headings in the ad itself. — [ELA assessment role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8224828); [Khan Kids bilingual role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8204881)
- The LINGUIST List Duolingo notice is not a careers-site template. Order: issue metadata; job location, title, rank, and salary; specialty areas and language codes; “About the Role”; “You will”; “You have”; “Exceptional candidates will have”; “Application Deadline: (Open until filled)”; application URL and contact. — [LINGUIST List 37.2709](https://linguistlist.org/issues/37/2709/)
- MasterClass AI Project Reviewer order: “Who we are”; a one-line invitation to apply even without exact experience; “Role Overview”; “What You Will Do”; “About You”; “You’re a Great Fit If You”; hourly range plus a geographic caveat; equal-employment paragraph. The form then requires a résumé and LinkedIn, with no further written prompt. — [AI Project Reviewer](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/6795250)
- MasterClass Creative Director, Certificates order: “Who We Are”; “About the Role”; “What You’ll Do”; “You Are”; “Qualifications”; a closed list of allowed states and two Canadian provinces; equal-employment paragraph; two salary caveats; “Salary Range.” The form requires LinkedIn and a portfolio or website, plus an optional portfolio password. — [Creative Director, Certificates](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/8097329)
- DataCamp Learning Solution Architect, as copied on Built In: a Built In “The Role” summary (explicitly “Summary Generated by Built In”); “About DataCamp”; “About the role + Responsibilities”; “Qualifications”; “Some of your key metrics will be”; “Why Datacamp?”; “Compensation.” Built In also stamps “Junior” and “130K-220K Annually” above the ad. The body itself is not labeled Junior. — [Learning Solution Architect on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/learning-solution-architect-ai-software-engineering/11543711)
- DataCamp Curriculum Manager on Built In: Built In summary; “About DataCamp”; “About the role” with a day-to-day list; “Qualifications”; “Bonus if you have the following”; “Why Datacamp?”. No salary heading appeared in the opened text. — [Curriculum Manager on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/curriculum-manager-data-science-and-data-engineering/6623937)
- DataCamp’s instructor PDF is a six-step timeline, not a job ad: Application Review; Create an Outline; Complete a Screencast; Committee Review; Offer; Contracting. Introductory line says application to hire should take about 4–6 weeks. — [Instructor hiring PDF](https://cdn.datacamp.com/DC%20Instructor%20Hiring%20Process.pdf)
- Google’s YouTube PLX ad order: location and fair-chance notes; “Minimum qualifications”; “Preferred qualifications”; “About the job”; a pay sentence; “Responsibilities”; then applicant privacy, EEO, accommodations, an English-proficiency rule, a ban on agency résumés, and an equity disclaimer. There is no separate benefits list; benefits are a link. — [Principal Lead job](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/102246871434437318-principal-lead-global-product-learning-experiences-youtube)
- Udacity Content Maintenance Specialist order: “About Us”; a catalog and maintenance explanation; “Understanding Our Learning Infrastructure”; “JOIN THE TEAM TODAY”; “Required skills/qualifications”; “Responsibilities”; “Why should you apply?”; “Compensation” (no number); Accenture equal-opportunity language; “Udacity's Values”; privacy link. The form adds degree, LinkedIn, and English-CV instructions. — [Content Maintenance Specialist](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/udacity/jobs/8694065002)
- Udacity Cybersecurity Technical Mentor order: “About Us”; a talent-pool paragraph; “You’ll be an excellent fit if you have”; “Mandatory Technical Skills – School of Cyber”; “Preferred”; “Responsibilities” split into 1:1 video calls, virtual technical deep-dive sessions, and group Q&A; “Why should you apply?”; a compensation paragraph with no number; the same Accenture legal block and values. — [Cybersecurity Technical Mentor](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/udacity/jobs/8535777002)
- Udacity’s mentor landing page, above the job list, uses: why mentors matter; four benefit labels; mentor-role tabs; qualities; “How to Become a Mentor” in four numbered steps; then “Open Positions.” — [Udacity become a mentor](https://www.udacity.com/become-a-mentor)
- The indexed, not-live Coursera learning-designer text began “About Coursera,” then “About the Role,” “Key Responsibilities,” and “Basic Qualifications.” Later headings were not in the snippet. — [Indexed Coursera LXD URL](http://job-boards.greenhouse.io/coursera/jobs/5777103004)

### Inferences
- The reusable A-to-Z skeleton in these ads is: scam or legal notice if any; one-paragraph company and learner; “the role” with term and location; bulleted work; bulleted proof you can do it; pay with an explicit “this number is not a promise” sentence; benefits only if the employer actually provides them; how to apply, including the work sample if one exists. Syllabus detail is written inside the role paragraph, not under its own heading.
- Values, EEO, and “who we are” often take more vertical space than the description of the thing the person will make.

### Gaps
- Full heading lists for Coursera’s closed learning-designer ad and for Duolingo’s rendered careers template were not available.
- Built In inserts its own summary and seniority label. Those are not DataCamp headings.

## How specific is the work the person will make?

### Takeaway
Specificity shows up as learner, language, subject band, and artifact type. Almost no live ad names a single lesson, a review SLA, or a weekly publishing quota. The subject is either a narrow band (grades 3–5 ELA; Hindi first) or a catalog the employer already owns.

### Cited Findings
- Khan Kids: the person builds and revises bilingual English and Spanish learning activities and direct assessments for PreK through 2nd grade, across early literacy, language, math, and executive function, for children ages 2 to 8. Named standards examples are Head Start ELOF, Common Core, and state PreK standards. Named artifacts include activities, assessment items, coverage maps that crosswalk practice to assessments, and audio requests. Review is concrete: editorial review in both languages, revision from child assessment data and psychometric analyses, and trying new items with children and educators in classrooms. The term is 24 months. The ad does not name a course title, a lesson count, or a sample lesson to submit. Spanish is both a work language and something “assessed during the hiring process.” — [Khan Kids bilingual role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8204881)
- Khan ELA assessments: the person writes assessment content from blueprints for grades 3–5 ELA, aligned to state standards and item-writing practice, and may try new item types that AI makes possible. The learner is a K–12 student taking an assessment, not a video viewer. Teammates are other ELA assessment creators plus product, design, and engineering. “Nice to have” is middle-school assessment experience. No state is named, no item count is given, and no sample item is requested in the visible questions. The form does ask whether the applicant has taught elementary ELA for at least two years, whether they have created assessment items, and what their assessment philosophy is. Term is 24 months. — [ELA assessment role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8224828)
- Duolingo, via the LINGUIST List text: the work starts with Hindi and can extend to other South Asian languages. The learner is a mobile language learner. Artifacts named are curriculum and learning design, human-in-the-loop AI content, and quality systems (guidelines, rubrics, review workflows) covering linguistic accuracy, difficulty, consistency, and brand voice. The person also recruits freelance educators, linguists, cultural consultants, and reviewers. Location is Pittsburgh only; the notice says no remote or other location will be accommodated. It does not set a CEFR level, a lesson count, a recording duty, or a sample lesson. Salary on that notice is $142,800–$193,200. — [LINGUIST List 37.2709](https://linguistlist.org/issues/37/2709/)
- Indexed Coursera learning-designer text, not re-opened live: India-based design of Coursera-produced short- and long-form content and support for APAC partners. Artifacts named in the snippet were learning outcomes, program outlines, sequenced learning journeys, storyboards, and/or assessment specifications, produced with subject-matter experts and vendors. Teams named included Teaching & Learning, Learning Analytics, Content Production Management, and Content Maintenance & Optimization. No subject, learner age, language, sample lesson, or review calendar was in the visible snippet. — [Indexed Coursera LXD URL](http://job-boards.greenhouse.io/coursera/jobs/5777103004)
- DataCamp instructor path: the subject is whichever course is on a public “Course Wishlist,” not a subject invented in the ad. The work sample is a course outline, which may be revised more than once, then a 3–5 minute screencast of one lesson from that outline. Search-index FAQ text says a course often takes about 10–15 hours a week for 10–12 weeks, that the first chapter is free to a very large learner base, and that the rest sits behind the subscription. That FAQ also says pay is either revenue share or a completion model, chosen by content type. — [Instructor hiring PDF](https://cdn.datacamp.com/DC%20Instructor%20Hiring%20Process.pdf); [DataCamp instructor page, search index](https://www.datacamp.com/teach)
- DataCamp Curriculum Manager: the territory is data science and data engineering content. Day to day is the content lifecycle, recruiting instructors, co-creating with them, reviewing as a learner for technical and pedagogical quality, and using learner feedback and engagement data to decide what to fix or add. Tools named are Python and SQL, plus agentic coding tools as a qualification. No course title, language of instruction, review turnaround, or sample lesson is specified. — [Curriculum Manager on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/curriculum-manager-data-science-and-data-engineering/6623937)
- DataCamp Learning Solution Architect: the person teaches live, virtually and onsite, and publishes custom courses, projects, certifications, and assessments on DataCamp’s platform for a customer’s roles and tools. Named audience slices are analyst, engineer, business user, and executive. Topic examples are machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, and deep learning. Success metrics in the ad are customer-satisfaction signals, repeat bookings, and professional services revenue. It does not name a public syllabus or a sample lesson. It does say the schedule is not 9-to-5 and that occasional travel is required. — [Learning Solution Architect on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/learning-solution-architect-ai-software-engineering/11543711)
- Google YouTube PLX: the learner is a Google Ads seller, not a YouTube viewer. The curriculum is modular and global, with regional teams allowed to add local context. Named artifacts are one-sheeters, videos, and “Yoodlis,” plus “standardized and tailorable product assets.” The production goal is to use AI so cycles move from months to weeks. Preferred experience names Gemini, NotebookLM, AI Studio, Adobe Creative Suite, and Google Vids, and fluency in the Google Ads ecosystem. No sample module is requested. English is required unless a posting says otherwise; this one does not waive it. Locations the applicant may prefer: New York, Chicago, Mountain View, Los Angeles, San Francisco, and London, with a possible Playa Vista seat for U.S. candidates. — [Principal Lead job](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/102246871434437318-principal-lead-global-product-learning-experiences-youtube)
- MasterClass AI Project Reviewer does not create a course. The person grades learner project submissions against rubrics already written, gives pass/fail plus written feedback, and compares human and AI judgments. Subject and course title are not named. Load is described as temporary, about 20 hours a week, freelance. The ad explicitly wants the reviewer not to use AI while evaluating or writing rationales. — [AI Project Reviewer](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/6795250)
- MasterClass Creative Director, Certificates shapes trailers, promos, social, and positioning for the Certificates product. It is marketing craft, not lesson authorship. A portfolio is required. — [Creative Director, Certificates](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/8097329)
- Udacity Content Maintenance Specialist maintains existing Generative AI courses. Topics listed include large language models, generative AI, computer vision, natural language processing, MLOps, and AI engineering on major clouds. The person updates text, screenshots, instructions, tutorials, exercises, projects, and in-browser workspaces when libraries or student feedback show the material is stale. What triggers the work is student satisfaction, lesson ratings, and page-level feedback. Scope, timeline, and which course are not in the ad; they are sent later when a project is offered from the contractor pool. The CV must be in English. — [Content Maintenance Specialist](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/udacity/jobs/8694065002)
- Udacity Cybersecurity Technical Mentor does not write the course. The person supports an existing School of Cybersecurity Nanodegree through 1:1 calls, slide deep-dives, and group Q&A. Mandatory skills are defensive and operational: fundamentals across network, application, and system security; threat detection, vulnerability assessment, and risk management; familiarity with SIEM, IDS/IPS, and firewalls; frameworks such as NIST and ISO 27001; incident response and monitoring; networking concepts; cloud security and data protection. Preferred lines add penetration-testing or ethical-hacking tools and certifications such as Security+, CEH, or CISSP. The ad does not include procedures. Project scope and pay are sent when Udacity contacts the pool. The form asks which cybersecurity Nanodegree and which languages the applicant can speak and write. — [Cybersecurity Technical Mentor](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/udacity/jobs/8535777002)

### Inferences
- When these companies are specific, they name the learner and the artifact, then stop. They do not invent the lesson outline in the job ad. DataCamp is the exception that forces the candidate to draft the outline, and only for a course the company already put on a wishlist.
- “Review cycle” is usually a principle (data, rubrics, student feedback, editorial review), not a calendar. Only Khan Kids names a setting for review (classrooms, psychometric analyses). Google names a desired change in cycle length (months to weeks) without a checkpoint list.
- A YouTube instructor ad in this style would name the viewer, the language, and the artifact (for example a short lesson plus a practice item), and would leave episode topics either to a wishlist or to a first outline the candidate submits. It would not pretend to know the full series.

### Gaps
- No opened posting specified a sample-lesson rubric, a script length beyond DataCamp’s 3–5 minute screencast, or how many revision rounds a finished course gets after hire.
- DataCamp’s current wishlist titles were not opened, so the exact subjects being hired for in October 2026 are unknown.
- Whether Duolingo requires a portfolio or sample lesson is not in the LINGUIST List text.

## Do they describe how they hire?

### Takeaway
Most staff ads stop at “apply.” The companies that hire outside instructors, DataCamp and Udacity’s mentor network, are the ones that publish the loop, and both loops include a work sample or an assessment. Portfolio appears when the job is judged on craft, not when it is judged on teaching years.

### Cited Findings
- Khan’s two opened ads do not list interview rounds. The forms collect a résumé, a cover letter or short answers, and yes/no screens. Kids asks which languages the applicant has written educational materials in, a description of Spanish use, whether they have developed or administered assessments for children under six, and which technical tools they have used. ELA asks for two years of elementary teaching, hands-on item writing, and an assessment philosophy. Both warn that recruiters only use @khanacademy.org and that Khan will not ask for money or equipment fees. Greenhouse’s own note says an automated tool may score skills, titles, years, dates, and employers. — [ELA assessment role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8224828); [Khan Kids bilingual role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8204881)
- DataCamp’s instructor PDF states a sequence of about 4–6 weeks: CV review against the wishlist course; an outline that can be sent back for revisions; a 3–5 minute screencast of one outlined lesson; a committee review of outline plus screencast; an offer conversation that first aligns the development timeline; then a contract. — [Instructor hiring PDF](https://cdn.datacamp.com/DC%20Instructor%20Hiring%20Process.pdf)
- DataCamp’s employee ads on Built In do not describe interviews, case studies, or a sample lesson. A separate, older DataCamp recruitment PDF in search results described a general employee loop (recruiter interview, hiring-manager interview, case study or technical test, founder review, references, background check). That PDF was not opened and is indexed as 7 July 2022, so it is not treated as the October 2026 process for these two jobs. — [Learning Solution Architect on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/learning-solution-architect-ai-software-engineering/11543711); [Curriculum Manager on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/curriculum-manager-data-science-and-data-engineering/6623937)
- Udacity’s mentor landing page publishes four steps: application; an online technical and interpersonal assessment; technical and interpersonal interviews; then training by enrolling the person in Nanodegree programs. The two opened contractor ads do not repeat those four steps. They do say people join a pool and are contacted later with project details. — [Udacity become a mentor](https://www.udacity.com/become-a-mentor)
- MasterClass Certificates creative director requires a portfolio or website. The AI reviewer requires LinkedIn and does not ask for a graded sample. Neither ad lists interview stages. The reviewer ad says people without the exact résumé should still apply. — [Creative Director, Certificates](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/8097329); [AI Project Reviewer](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/6795250)
- The Google PLX ad does not describe interviews. It links a general “How we hire” page, which was not opened. Applying is a button on the job. — [Principal Lead job](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/102246871434437318-principal-lead-global-product-learning-experiences-youtube)
- A search index of Duolingo’s careers page said the company evaluates craft, leadership, and citizenship, starts with application review, and later includes a conversation about what matters to the candidate and which other opportunities they are considering. The full step list was not on a page that rendered in this session, and it was not inside the Hindi job text. — [Duolingo careers search index](https://www.duolingo.com/careers); [LINGUIST List 37.2709](https://linguistlist.org/issues/37/2709/)

### Inferences
- A complete instructor posting in this market either (a) says nothing about interviews and screens with a short written answer, or (b) if the person will originate a course, asks for an outline and a few minutes of teaching before any offer.
- Publishing “we will tell you the steps later” is less common than simply omitting the loop.

### Gaps
- No opened page listed the number of interviews, who attends, or whether a live teaching demo happens after the application for staff roles.
- Duolingo’s typical loop could not be confirmed from a rendered page.
- Google’s general hiring page was not opened, so Google’s interview shape is unknown from this pass.

## What do they refuse to pin down, or postpone?

### Takeaway
They publish a pay band or they explicitly postpone the number. They almost never postpone the subject so completely that any topic would do. What they postpone is the exact offer inside a band, the contract type for instructors, and, for contractor pools, which course and which week.

### Cited Findings
- Khan prints a band of $77,600–$87,300 USD, or $104,862–$117,969 CAD, for both opened content roles, then says the range is “a general guideline only” and the offer depends on internal pay equity, skills, experience, qualifications, and job-market data. Parental leave is “generous” with no duration. Time off is “as needed,” plus 8 named wellness days in 2026. They do not postpone location rules: continental US, Hawaii, and Canada only, San Mateo or remote inside that set. — [ELA assessment role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8224828); [Khan Kids bilingual role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8204881)
- Duolingo’s repost prints $142,800–$193,200 and refuses remote work outright. It leaves the close date as “open until filled.” It does not describe offer negotiations. — [LINGUIST List 37.2709](https://linguistlist.org/issues/37/2709/)
- MasterClass prints $75–$125 per hour for the reviewer and $165,000–$190,000 for the certificates creative director, then says the printed range is the NYC/CA/WA figure and has not been adjusted for other geographies. The creative-director ad also says they may hire someone less experienced or more senior than the description, and if so “the updated salary range will be communicated with you as a candidate.” Benefits are “medical, dental, vision, flexible PTO, and more,” with equity mentioned but not sized. — [AI Project Reviewer](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/6795250); [Creative Director, Certificates](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/8097329)
- DataCamp’s instructor FAQ index says the pay model is predetermined by content type and “discussed in more detail during the contracting stage.” The PDF says that before the official offer, Instructor Success aligns the development timeline and then walks through contract type and general details. Employee ads that were opened do publish ranges for the architect role: base $130,000–$190,000 and total OTE $145,000–$220,000, varying by approved job level. The curriculum-manager copy that was opened did not show a number. — [DataCamp instructor page, search index](https://www.datacamp.com/teach); [Instructor hiring PDF](https://cdn.datacamp.com/DC%20Instructor%20Hiring%20Process.pdf); [Learning Solution Architect on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/learning-solution-architect-ai-software-engineering/11543711)
- Google prints US $171,000–$247,000 plus a 20% bonus target, plus equity, plus benefits, then says individual pay depends on skills, experience, and education. A later disclaimer says equity grants are discretionary, not guaranteed, and not an acquired right. Benefits are not listed; the ad links out. The ad also refuses unsolicited agency résumés. — [Principal Lead job](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/102246871434437318-principal-lead-global-product-learning-experiences-youtube)
- Both opened Udacity contractor ads say compensation “varies” by location, role, skill, and experience, and that local law requires them to provide a reasonable range, but neither page stated a number. Both say project scope and timing are shared when a need arises, not in the posting. U.S. applicants must already have work authorization that will not require visa sponsorship. The maintenance ad also refuses a non-English CV. — [Content Maintenance Specialist](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/udacity/jobs/8694065002); [Cybersecurity Technical Mentor](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/udacity/jobs/8535777002)
- None of the opened ads contains a sentence that says the company will not invent curriculum, topics, or claims. The operational version of that limit is elsewhere: DataCamp hires against a wishlist; Udacity assigns maintenance from an existing catalog; Khan names the grade band and standards; Duolingo names Hindi first. — same URLs as the role findings above

### Inferences
- Honest “discussed later” lines that a small channel can copy without pretending to be a large employer: the exact offer inside a stated range; which of two contract types applies; the production calendar after a sample is accepted; which existing video gets updated first.
- A line that would be false if copied without the fact behind it: “no remote,” “open until filled,” “we will assess your Spanish,” or “equity is discretionary.”

### Gaps
- Coursera’s live board did not show pay philosophy for a content role because no content role was open.
- DataCamp’s instructor pay amounts, as distinct from the choice between revenue share and a completion fee, were not on the opened PDF.

## Which sections would be dishonest if a small channel copied them?

### Takeaway
The dishonest copy is the benefits and scale block: retirement match, insurance, parental leave, wellness days, bonus target, equity, retreats, equipment budgets, and learner counts in the millions. The honest, reusable part is the work description, the sample, and a pay sentence that matches what the channel will actually pay.

### Cited Findings
- Khan’s perks block offers competitive salary, paid time off “as needed,” 8 wellness days in 2026, remote-first work inside named countries, “generous” parental leave, affinity groups, a 401(k) with 4% match, and medical, dental, vision, and life insurance. The company blurb cites more than 181 million registered learners. Copying that list, or the $77.6k–$87.3k band, would describe Khan, not a small channel. — [Khan Kids bilingual role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8204881)
- Google’s pay line is $171,000–$247,000 plus a 20% bonus target plus equity, for a role that influences Director-level and VP-level stakeholders and may sit in several global offices. Benefits are a separate Google benefits page. Copying the bonus, equity, or campus list would be false for a channel that does not have them. The equity paragraph also says grants are not guaranteed; copying only the upside and dropping that sentence would misstate even Google’s ad. — [Principal Lead job](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/102246871434437318-principal-lead-global-product-learning-experiences-youtube)
- MasterClass’s opened ads assume equity plus medical, dental, vision, and flexible PTO, and they lean on a brand description of 200+ instructors and celebrity classrooms. The reviewer rate of $75–$125 an hour is tied to a location-based pay strategy. A small channel that pasted either the rate or the celebrity “who we are” block would be describing MasterClass. — [AI Project Reviewer](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/6795250); [Creative Director, Certificates](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass/jobs/8097329)
- DataCamp’s architect ad lists flexible time off, a 401(k) with matching, medical, dental, vision, and life insurance, paid sick time as required by law, equity, a yearly learning budget, international retreats, and a yearly IT-equipment budget, beside a claim of over 20 million learners and 6,000+ companies including 80% of the Fortune 1000. The curriculum-manager copy repeats retreats, a learning budget, and an equipment budget, and names partner instructors from organizations such as Microsoft, Hugging Face, and AWS. Those are employer facts, not a template. — [Learning Solution Architect on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/learning-solution-architect-ai-software-engineering/11543711); [Curriculum Manager on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/curriculum-manager-data-science-and-data-engineering/6623937)
- Udacity’s opened contractor ads do not list health insurance. What would still be false if copied is the Accenture employment machinery: visa rules, the equal-opportunity statement of a global consultancy, and “Udacity, an Accenture company.” The mentor landing benefits that are safe to distinguish, because they are not insurance, are flexible remote work, recognition, a chance to inform content, and a mentor community. Even those should be claimed only if the channel actually offers them. — [Content Maintenance Specialist](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/udacity/jobs/8694065002); [Udacity become a mentor](https://www.udacity.com/become-a-mentor)
- Duolingo’s reposted salary band and the Pittsburgh-only rule are facts about that role. Copying the band, or a benefits package from older Duolingo content-designer ads that mentioned equity, would not describe a small channel. Those older content-designer ads were aggregator pages and were not opened as live October 2026 postings. — [LINGUIST List 37.2709](https://linguistlist.org/issues/37/2709/)
- Khan’s scam notice is specific to the @khanacademy.org domain. Copying it unchanged would send applicants to the wrong rule. The underlying idea, “we will not ask you for money,” is not a benefits claim. — [ELA assessment role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8224828)

### Inferences
- For a small YouTube instructor posting, the sections to write from scratch are pay, time off, equipment, and reach. The sections that can follow the large-company pattern without copying their facts are: who the learner is, what artifacts are due, how a sample lesson is judged, and what is decided only after the sample.

### Gaps
- Exact benefit booklets were not opened. “And more,” “generous,” and “learn more about benefits” hide items that might or might not include disability leave, stipends, or sabbaticals. Those hidden items should not be guessed.
