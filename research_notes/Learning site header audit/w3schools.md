## What is noisy or hostile versus actually useful for someone who came to read one lesson?

Noisy: every account, certificate, app, and ad control that repeats from the bar through the example and the footer. ## What should Chaduvuko take and refuse?

### Takeaway
Take three W3Schools mechanisms that help a logged-out person read the next page. Refuse three that spend the first screen on an account, an app, or a strip that does not fit a phone.

### Cited Findings
Take:
- The **❮ Previous / Next ❯ pair** (`.nextprev` on Python Strings). Two real links, repeated at the top and bottom of the lesson, green Next at 45px tall, no account, and they name the neighbor pages (`python_challenges_casting.asp`, `python_strings_slicing.asp`). Chaduvuko already has Previous and Next in the bar; this is the pattern of also leaving them at the end of the article so a phone reader does not scroll back to a header. — [Python Strings HTML](https://www.w3schools.com/python/python_strings.asp); measured 2026-10-09
- The **Tutorials topic cards' one-line blurbs** ("HTML / The building blocks of every webpage", "Python / Popular programming language…", and the same shape on `/tutorials/index.php`). That is the useful part of the Tutorials menu, and it matches Tracks that already carry one-line blurbs. Do not take the certificate card or the Adventure card that sit in the same panel. — measured 2026-10-09; [tutorials index](https://www.w3schools.com/tutorials/index.php)
- The **logged-out search field** `#tnb-google-search-input` (placeholder "Search...", 16px type, green focus outline) and the suggestion list that named "Python Strings" before any login. Keep Chaduvuko's search as a field, including on a phone. The lesson to copy is "suggestions without an account," not the 36×36 icon it collapses into under 616px. — [topnav CSS v1.0.144](https://www.w3schools.com/lib/topnav/main.v1.0.144.css); measured 2026-10-09

Refuse:
- The **black `#subtopnav` language strip**. It is a second fixed row, 33px tall, scrollbar hidden, about 4000px of names, and at 320px it clips the current row into "ASCRIPT" and a half-visible JAVA. It does not carry the lesson title or "N of M". Chaduvuko's one 64px bar already says which track you are in. — measured 2026-10-09; [Python Strings HTML](https://www.w3schools.com/python/python_strings.asp)
- The **right side of `#pagetop`**: Sign In, Upgrade, Paid Courses, Spaces, and the NEW Adventure app badge. On a phone those collapse into a 36px person icon and a 50px lynx, and the Menu that replaces Tutorials continues the same pitch (W3Schools app, Spaces, Plus, Academy). That cluster is why the bar cannot say which lesson you are on. — [lesson-390-menu.png](research_notes/Learning%20site%20header%20audit/w3schools-shots/lesson-390-menu.png); [topnav CSS v1.0.144](https://www.w3schools.com/lib/topnav/main.v1.0.144.css)
- The **"Sign in to track progress" link and the homepage XP card** (12,560 / "XP this week 850" / "Day streak 7" / Sign in), plus the in-example **"Get your own Python Server"** button. They impersonate progress and a code action for a reader who has neither an account nor a need to host. Anonymous `localStorage` on these loads kept a theme, not a lesson. Chaduvuko's progress stays in the browser and should not be a login wall or a fake streak. — [homepage](https://www.w3schools.com/); [Python Strings HTML](https://www.w3schools.com/python/python_strings.asp); measured 2026-10-09

### Inferences
- Copy the wayfinding that stays inside the lesson. Do not copy the site map that sits on top of it, and do not copy anything whose job is to open an account, a plan, or an app.

### Gaps
- None for this recommendation. The three takes and three refuses were each seen in the DOM or in a 2026-10-09 screenshot.
