# GeeksforGeeks header, navigation, and article chrome

Observed 2026-10-09 by loading the live site in headless Chrome at 1440×1000, 390×844, and 320×700, and by reading the HTML, `__NEXT_DATA__`, the header CSS, and the `_app` bundle those pages ship. The site build id in the homepage payload is `2026-10-09T05-20-51-701Z`. The tutorial used is the leaf article [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/) (final URL unchanged; title “Python Introduction - GeeksforGeeks”; on-page “Last Updated : 19 Sep, 2026”). The Python hub [Python Tutorial](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/python-programming-language-tutorial/) is a separate index page, not the article measured below.

## What is in the header on the homepage and on a tutorial?

### Takeaway
On the homepage and on the Python article, the desktop header is two stacked rows, not one: a 50px bar (logo, search, Courses, Tutorials, Interview Prep, theme, Sign In) and a 44px horizontally scrolling link row that changes per page. Tutorials, courses, and practice are separate destinations, but practice and courses are also stuffed into that second row, and the search-results URL swaps the whole top menu.

### Cited Findings
- Homepage top row, after hydration at 1440px, reads logo, search icon, “Courses”, “Tutorials”, “Interview Prep”, a theme button whose accessible name is “Toggle GFG Theme”, and “Sign In”. The bar (`#topMainHeader` / `.headerContainer`) measures 1440×50 at y=0. “Courses” is about 97×24, “Tutorials” 103×24, “Interview Prep” 141×24. — [GeeksforGeeks homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/)
- CSS sets that bar to `height: 50px` and the row under it (`.outisdeMainContainerSubheader`, the spelling on the site) to `min-height: 40px`. In Chrome the second row measured 44px tall, and a sticky wrapper around both measured 95px. — [Header CSS](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/css/9767f1e9899c3935.css); [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/)
- Hovering “Tutorials” opened a single column: Python, Java, ML & Data Science, Programming Languages, Web Development, CS Subjects, DevOps, Software and Tools, School Learning. Those strings match the homepage `headerData` payload. The same payload’s other two menus were not re-hovered; they are Courses → “DSA / Placements”, “DA / Data Science & AI”, “Development”, “DevOps Course”, “All Courses” (parent link `https://practice.geeksforgeeks.org/courses/`), and Interview Prep → “Interview Corner”, “Practice DSA”, “GfG 160” (`/courses/gfg-160-series`), “Problem of the Day”. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/)
- The hydrated homepage second row is ten nowrap links: DSA, Practice Problems (`/explore`), C, C++, Java, Python, JavaScript, Data Science, Machine Learning, Courses (`/courses`). — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/)
- The homepage `__NEXT_DATA__` `subHeaderData` lists those ten plus Linux, DevOps, SQL, Web Development, System Design, and Aptitude. The raw HTML response also included Linux and DevOps in the `<ul>`. Those extra labels were not in the hydrated DOM in the Chrome session. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/)
- Under the header, the homepage is not a lesson index. It says “Start Your Learning Today!” and shows three chips: “Placement 360”, “Data Science 360”, “Full Stack Dev”, each `target="_blank"` to a `/courses/` URL. Then an “Explore” card grid and a “Courses” block of paid cards (“Explore now”, star ratings, “488k+ interested Geeks”). — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/)
- On the Python article the top row is the same three menus plus Sign In. The second row changes to Python Tutorial, Data Types, Interview Questions, Examples, Quizzes, DSA Python, Data Science, NumPy, Pandas, Practice. “Practice” here points at an article about the practice platform (`/dsa/geeksforgeeks-practice-best-online-coding-platform/`), not at `/explore`. The article’s `subHeaderData` also lists Django, Flask, and Projects; Django and Flask were in the raw HTML, and none of those three were in the hydrated link list. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- The Python hub is a third page type: same Courses / Tutorials / Interview Prep top row, but its second row matches the article’s Python links (through Flask in the fetched HTML) and the body is a link directory, not the lesson. — [Python Tutorial hub](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/python-programming-language-tutorial/)
- `/search/?gq=binary%20search` does not reuse that header. Its payload and HTML use four top items: Courses (adds “GATE Prep”), Tutorials (adds DSA, Interview Corner, GATE), Practice (“Earn Google Badge”, “Practice Coding Problems”, “Problem of the Day”, “Connect 1:1 with Experts”), and Jobs (“Apply Now!”, “Post Jobs”, “Jobs Updates”, “Share Interview Experience”). Title of that response: “You searched for binary search - GeeksforGeeks”. — [Search results](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/search/?gq=binary%20search)

### Inferences
- A large menu here is three competing catalogs. Written tutorials, paid courses, and practice problems are separate products, then practice and courses are repeated as chips, and the search URL throws the catalog out and inserts Jobs. That is not a map of one curriculum.
- The second row looks like wayfinding and behaves like a promotional shelf. On the article it does not name the lesson you are reading (“Python Introduction” is not one of the chips) and it does not offer Previous, Next, or “lesson N of M”.
- The mismatch between `subHeaderData` (16 homepage links) and the hydrated row (10) means even the published list is not stable. Copying the labels would copy an overflow dump, not an information architecture.

### Gaps
- Courses and Interview Prep were not opened with a hover in Chrome. Their child labels are from the same homepage `headerData` object that the Tutorials hover matched exactly. Not a second visual check.
- No India-IP variant was loaded. Article markup uses `outsideIN` / `LeftBarOutsideIndia` class names, so a signed-in or India response could move the “Explore” list. This network got the outside-India document.

## At 390px and 320px, what stays visible, what is buried, and what covers the article?

### Takeaway
At both 390 and 320 the top row keeps only the hamburger, the logo, and the search icon. Courses, Tutorials, Interview Prep, Sign In, and dark mode move into a 300px drawer. The second link row stays, clipped, with no visible scrollbar. The article text is not covered by a sticky ad or a bottom bar; the drawer, when open, covers almost the whole phone, and Escape does not close it. Most header hit targets are under 44px, and the main menus are not in the tab order.

### Cited Findings
- At 390×844 and 320×700 the homepage top row has no `.headerMainListItem`. The hamburger (`.hamburgerMenu`) measures 35×35 at x=15, y=0, `tabIndex` -1, no `role`, no `aria-label`. The search icon (`.gs-toggle-icon`) measures 32×32 (x=303 at 390, x=233 at 320). CSS agrees: hamburger `height: 35px; width: 35px`, search toggle `height: 32px; width: 32px`. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/); [header CSS](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/css/9767f1e9899c3935.css)
- The second row is still there, y=51, height 44, `overflow-x: auto` and `scrollbar-width: none`. At 390 the homepage links that start past the viewport include JavaScript at x=404, Data Science at x=489, Machine Learning at x=587, Courses at x=715. At 320, Java starts at x=292 and is cut by the 320px edge. Anchor boxes are about 21px tall; the “C” anchor is 8×21. The parent `li` is 44px tall, but the `<a>` is the control. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/); [header CSS](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/css/9767f1e9899c3935.css)
- Opening the hamburger shows a panel from x=0, y=51, width 300, full viewport height. Visible labels: “Sign In”, then “Courses”, “Tutorials”, and “Interview Prep”, each with a chevron. A dark-mode icon sits below them. The page behind is dimmed. At 320px the panel leaves about 20px of the page; at 390px about 90px. Sign In inside the closed drawer measures 200×44 and sits off-canvas (x=-250) until the drawer opens. Desktop Sign In is 73×37. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/)
- After Escape, the drawer stayed open at both 390 and 320 (`headerSidebarWrapperOpen` still had width greater than 40). The hamburger is a `<span>` with `tabIndex: -1`, so it is not a Tab stop. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/); drawer markup in [the `_app` bundle](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/chunks/pages/_app-59349147fe26e483.js)
- Desktop Tab order from the top of the homepage: logo link (outline `auto 1px`, blue), the search field (placeholder “Search...”, 172×25, outline style `none`), the theme button (32×32, outline `none`), Sign In (outline `none`), then the second-row links (outline `auto 1px` on the small text box). Courses, Tutorials, and Interview Prep did not appear in the first twelve tabs. In the bundle they are `<span>`s toggled by `onMouseOver` and `onClick`, not links, and a document click listener closes them. No Escape handler for that menu turned up in the bundle; a separate menu helper hides on Escape, but that path is not this header. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/); [`_app` bundle](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/chunks/pages/_app-59349147fe26e483.js)
- On the article at 390, the visible second row is “Python Tutorial”, “Data Types”, and “Interview Questions”, then the rest is clipped. The h1 “Python Introduction” starts at y=115. “Last Updated : 19 Sep, 2026” sits on one line, with comment, pencil, and a three-dot icon on the right. At 320 the date wraps: “Last Updated :” on one line and “19 Sep, 2026” on the next, and the second row shows “Python Tutorial”, “Data Types”, and a clipped “Interview”. No bottom bar and no app-install banner were on screen. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- Fixed layers on the phone article were the off-canvas drawer (x=-300 when closed) and the sticky topic row (`.mainSubHeaderDiv`, y=50, height 45). An in-article video iframe (`https://aa.geeksforgeeks.org/instream/video.html`) sits in the flow, not over the title: about 350×380 at y=1417 on 390px, and 280×380 at y=1624 on 320px. A reCAPTCHA iframe is in the DOM near the bottom edge and does not cover the h1. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- On the homepage, scrolling to y=900 left both rows stuck (header y=0, second row y=51). On the article, scrolling to y=700 moved the 50px primary bar to y=-700 and left the topic row stuck at y=1. The primary bar hides on scroll only on the article. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/); [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)

### Inferences
- The phone header saves horizontal space by deleting the only labels that say what the site is. What remains (logo, search, a nameless 35px icon) does not tell a new reader that tutorials, courses, and practice are different things. Those three words are one extra tap away, behind a control that keyboard users cannot focus and that Escape will not dismiss.
- 44px is the usual minimum tap target. The hamburger (35), search icon (32), theme control (32), desktop Sign In (37px tall), second-row anchors (~21, and “C” at 8×21), and the primary menu items (~24px tall) all miss it. The drawer’s Sign In button is the one control that meets 44px, and it exists to open an account.
- Nothing in the phone article chrome is a lesson navigator. The sticky row still shows sibling hubs, and the title block wastes a line at 320px on the date wrap. The reading column itself is clear until the in-flow video ad.

### Gaps
- Child rows inside the phone drawer were not expanded, so the chevron contents were not seen painted. The bundle builds those rows from the same `headerData` children as the desktop menu.
- Escape was not retested against the desktop Courses/Tutorials dropdown, because the click used to open Tutorials did not leave a measurable open menu before the hover pass. Backdrop click is what the bundle uses to close menus.
- The results modal never painted in headless Chrome, so Escape-to-close-search was not observed. See the search section.

## How does an article show where you are, and does that help or fight reading?

### Takeaway
The Python article does not show lesson position. There is no visible breadcrumb, no Previous/Next, no “N of M”, and the current Explore row is not highlighted. Where-you-are is a sticky row of sibling hubs. Desktop reading is a ~792px column between two 300px ad rails; the curriculum list starts after the article.

### Cited Findings
- H1 is “Python Introduction”. Beside it: “Last Updated : 19 Sep, 2026”, plus comment, pencil (“improve” in the class names), and a three-dot control. No Previous or Next control was in the DOM (`nextBtns` empty). The bottom row that uses a “NextArticle” class name renders the word “Comment” and an empty sibling div. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- There is no visible breadcrumb. The only breadcrumb is JSON-LD: item 1 “Python” (`/category/python/`), item 2 the slug `introduction-to-python`, not the title “Python Introduction”. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- At 1440 the article column measures x=323, width 792. The left rail (`.sidebar_wrapper`) is x=3, width 300, height about 2623, and its slots are `GFG_AD_Leftsidebar_300x250_chained_1` through `_3` plus a 300×600 slot. On first load those first three slots contained Google ad iframes (`/27823234/GFG_Leftsidebar_...`) at about 250×600. The right rail (`#secondary`) is x=1135, width 300, height about 2575, with slots named `GFG_AD_Desktop_RightSideBar_ATF_300x250_2`, a three-slide carousel, `ATF_300x600`, `MTF_300x250`, `BTF_Sticky_300x250`, `Docked_160x600`, and `BTFdocked_300x600`. After scrolling, both rails showed a book ad, “The Elements of Marie Curie” / “Read now”, not related lessons. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- The curriculum block is `#similar-reads`, headed “Explore”, and it starts around y=2249, below the article, not in the left rail. Groups include Python Fundamentals, Python Data Structures, Advanced Python, Data Science with Python, Web Development with Python, Python Practice, and Courses. Each link has a reading time (“Introduction” “1 min read”, “Variables” “4 min read”). The link for this article has `font-weight: 400` and a transparent background. It is not marked as the current lesson. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- In the article body, a video unit titled “Python Introduction” is followed by a “Visit Course” control (`visit` count 1). On the 390px screenshot the code sample’s run/copy toolbar sits on top of the first code line. H2s in the loaded article include “Hello World Program”, “Python Introduction”, “Python Standard and Implementations”, “How Python Programs are Executed”, and “Python Programming Terminology”. There is no table-of-contents component in the HTML (no “Table of Contents” string). — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- `articleLeftbarData` in the page payload is the same Explore tree (Python Fundamentals has nine children, each with `reading_time`). `promotionalCtaDataTop`, `promotionalCtaDataBottom`, `rightBarCourseCarouselData`, `textBasedAd`, and `practiceBannerData` were null in this response. The ads and the “Visit Course” block are still rendered from other slots. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)

### Inferences
- The Explore list would be useful if it were the left rail and marked the current lesson. Placed after a long article, with the current row unstyled, it is a second table of contents you meet when you are done. It fights nothing only because the reader has already left.
- The sticky topic row fights the lesson. It stays on screen while the title scrolls away, and its labels (Quizzes, NumPy, Practice, Data Science) are not the next paragraph. A reader cannot tell that they are inside “Python Fundamentals” rather than on a random Python URL.
- The desktop rails fight reading directly. About 600px of a 1440px viewport is ad inventory beside a 792px column, and one right slot is named `BTF_Sticky`. On the phone the rails drop away and the column is full width, which is the only layout on this page that treats the text as the product. The in-flow video and “Visit Course” then put the course pitch back into the lesson.

### Gaps
- Whether the India layout pins Explore to the left rail was not observed. Class names say this document is the outside-India variant.
- Ad creatives depend on the ad network. This session filled the rails with a book ad and the body with a GFG video plus “Visit Course”. Another viewer could get different creatives in the same slots. The slot ids are part of the page source.

## How does search behave?

### Takeaway
Header search is one ungrouped list of Google custom-search hits, not catalogs for tutorials vs courses vs practice. A hit whose path is `/problems` or `/explore` gets a “Problem” tag. The query is sent with `type=premium`. The results overlay did not open in headless Chrome; the bundle and a live call to the same endpoint are the evidence for what the list contains.

### Cited Findings
- Clicking the header search icon in Chrome opened an inline field, placeholder “Search...”, measuring 172×25. It did not open a modal. Submitting “binary search” through that field (native value set, `input` event, `requestSubmit`) produced no `.gs-modal-body`, no result rows, and no `gfgsearch` request. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/)
- The shipped search component uses placeholder “Search...” in the header and “Search for anything” inside the modal. Submit calls `{GFG_UD_URL}gfgsearch/?` plus the query string and `&search_type=google`, with `page`, `sort` defaulting to `relevance`, and `type` hardcoded to `premium`. If page 1 is empty it falls back to `api/v1/global-search?products=articles&articles_count=50&query=`. `GFG_UD_URL` in the bundle is `https://utilapi.geeksforgeeks.org/api/`. The modal meta row includes a literal `false`, so the sort control is not rendered. Backdrop click clears the term and closes the modal. A “Go Premium” link points at `https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/geeksforgeeks-premium-subscription`. — [`_app` bundle](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/chunks/pages/_app-59349147fe26e483.js)
- `GET https://utilapi.geeksforgeeks.org/api/gfgsearch/?page=1&sort=relevance&type=premium&query=binary%20search&search_type=google` returned `customsearch#search` with 10 items. The first eight mix a tutorial (`/dsa/binary-search/`), a practice problem (`/problems/who-will-win-1587115621/1`, title “Binary Search | Practice”), and more articles (Python bisect, BST, Java ArrayList, JavaScript). There are no group headings. — [gfgsearch API](https://utilapi.geeksforgeeks.org/api/gfgsearch/?page=1&sort=relevance&type=premium&query=binary%20search&search_type=google)
- Each row is a thumbnail plus title plus a description built as the first 10 characters of `last_updated` or `post_modified`, then the snippet. If the path matches `/explore` or `/problems`, the title gets a button whose text is “Problem”. CSS hides `.gs-single-result-problem-text` at `max-width: 600px`, so the word “Problem” is dropped on a phone and the arrow icons remain. Result images are `display: none` at `max-width: 565px`. — [`_app` bundle](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/chunks/pages/_app-59349147fe26e483.js); [header CSS](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/css/9767f1e9899c3935.css)
- The row component reads `page_map.cse_thumbnail` or `img`. The live Google payload uses `pagemap`, not `page_map`. Thumbnails in the UI can fall through to the default GFG image `gfg_200x200-min.png`. — [`_app` bundle](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/chunks/pages/_app-59349147fe26e483.js); [gfgsearch API](https://utilapi.geeksforgeeks.org/api/gfgsearch/?page=1&sort=relevance&type=premium&query=binary%20search&search_type=google)
- A separate results URL exists: `/search/?gq=`. The header on that document is the Courses / Tutorials / Practice / Jobs variant, not the article header. The pre-JS HTML does not include the hit list. — [Search results](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/search/?gq=binary%20search)

### Inferences
- Search does not know about Chaduvuko-style tracks. It asks Google for pages and, only as a fallback, for `products=articles`. Courses are absent from that request. Practice shows up only when a result URL happens to live under `/problems` or `/explore`, and the phone layout hides the word that says so.
- `type=premium` on every header query is a monetized filter baked into the request, not a reader control. Combined with the Go Premium link in the modal, search is a second place to sell, not a way to resume a lesson.
- The inline field is 25px tall and has `outline-style: none`. It fails the same target-size and focus tests as the rest of the bar.

### Gaps
- The results overlay was not seen painted, so grouping, empty state, and Escape behavior are from the bundle and the API, not from a screenshot of the modal. If a later experiment turned the modal off, the live click is the thing that matched this session and the bundle is the thing that still describes the intended UI.
- Suggestion dropdowns (`tracks-section`, suggestion box) are referenced in CSS and forced `display: none` under 1091px. In the bundle the suggestions child is a literal `false`. No suggestion list was observed.

## Which patterns are useful for a written-lesson site, and which exist to monetize or pad SEO?

### Takeaway
Keep the three GeeksforGeeks pieces that help someone read the next lesson. Refuse the three that exist to sell a course, open an account, or wrap the lesson in a second bar and a drawer. Chaduvuko already has a single 64px bar with track, module, title, “lesson N of M”, Previous, and Next. GeeksforGeeks’s article header has none of those, and its chrome is mostly a storefront.

### Cited Findings
- Useful, and small: Explore rows carry “1 min read”, “2 min read”, “4 min read” next to the lesson title, from `reading_time` in `articleLeftbarData`. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- Useful, and small: a result whose path is `/problems` or `/explore` is marked “Problem” in the same list as articles, instead of a second search engine. The word is hidden under 600px. — [`_app` bundle](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/chunks/pages/_app-59349147fe26e483.js)
- Useful on a phone: at 390 and 320 the lesson column is full width. The 300px rails are not on screen, and the h1 is on screen under about 115px of header. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/)
- Monetized: homepage chips “Placement 360”, “Data Science 360”, “Full Stack Dev”; course cards with “Explore now”; article rails reserved as `GFG_AD_*`; in-article “Visit Course”; search `type=premium` and a “Go Premium” link; Sign In in the bar and as the first drawer item. — [homepage](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/); [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/); [`_app` bundle](https://assets.geeksforgeeks.org/gfg-assets/_next/static/chunks/pages/_app-59349147fe26e483.js)
- SEO padding, not navigation: the BreadcrumbList uses the slug `introduction-to-python` and is not shown. The second row and the Explore list repeat the same Python hubs in two more places. The search-page header adds Jobs and “Connect 1:1 with Experts”, which are not the lesson. — [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/); [Search results](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/search/?gq=binary%20search)

### Inferences
- GeeksforGeeks can afford a 95px sticky stack and a 300px drawer because the header’s job is to route people into courses, practice, jobs, and ads. A free lesson site with no account does not have that job. The second row fails as a curriculum even on its own terms: it does not contain the current lesson, it does not highlight it, and on a 320px screen it shows two and a half labels.
- The article’s decision to drop the primary bar on scroll and keep the topic chips is the pattern to avoid. It hides the stable controls and keeps the noisy ones. The homepage, by contrast, keeps both rows, which is 95px of chrome that never goes away.

### Gaps
- No logged-in session was used. The drawer source adds “My Profile” only when `isLoggedIn` is true. Signed-out behavior is what was measured.
- Paid-course checkout and the Sign In modal were not opened. The button label, the 73×37 size, and the auth endpoints in the bundle are the evidence. The modal’s fields were not recorded.

### Take
1. Take the **“N min read” text on the Explore rows** of [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/). Put that on Chaduvuko’s existing track and module menus. Do not add Explore as a second rail or a block under the lesson.
2. Take the **“Problem” tag** in header search results (the label the bundle adds when the URL is under `/problems` or `/explore`). One search list, with a badge that says lesson vs practice vs interview. Do not take the Google custom-search request, the `type=premium` flag, or the hidden-on-phone label.
3. Take the **phone article column**: at 390 and 320 the lesson is full width, with the title on screen and no side rail. Keep Chaduvuko’s lesson like that. Do not take the 1440px three-column version.

### Refuse
1. Refuse the **left and right ad rails** (`GFG_AD_Leftsidebar_*`, `GFG_AD_Desktop_RightSideBar_*`, including `BTF_Sticky`) and the in-article **“Visit Course”** block on [Python Introduction](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/introduction-to-python/). They sit beside and inside the lesson and are there to sell.
2. Refuse **Sign In**, the search modal’s **Go Premium** link, and the homepage chips **Placement 360**, **Data Science 360**, and **Full Stack Dev**. Chaduvuko has no accounts and no paid upsell, and these are the header’s real calls to action.
3. Refuse the **two-row header** (the 50px bar plus the 44px `containerSubheader`) and the article behavior that **scrolls the primary bar away while the chip row sticks**. Also refuse the **35×35 `hamburgerMenu` drawer**: it has no accessible name, it is not in the tab order, Escape leaves it open, and it is a second layer on top of a bar Chaduvuko should not split. One 64px bar that already shows track, module, title, lesson N of M, Previous, and Next is the opposite of this header, and it should stay that way.
