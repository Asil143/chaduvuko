# Borrow their order, leave their facts

Famous companies structure a careers index as a dense list of titles and places, and a single job as a walk from the title through the work to a legal close. **Stripe and Anthropic keep the brand essay on a parent URL and the rows on a separate index; Notion and Linear put the essay on the same URL as the list; Google, Amazon, and Microsoft put the posting inside or beside a searchable catalog.** On the job, the stable sequence is the title, a few facts, an Apply control, then who the company or learner is, what the person will make, and what they must already be able to do. Pay is a paragraph or an optional widget, not the spine of the page, and **live posts at Per Scholas, Notion, Udacity, and Linear ship with no numeric range**. A small education company can use that order without inventing a salary, a city, or full-time versus contract, because those three are fields the applicant systems allow to be broad, non-numeric, or absent. What cannot travel is someone else's benefits catalog, offices, equity, and scale. Pages opened on 13 October 2026, and named below, are Stripe, Notion, Linear, Vercel, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Khan Academy, Coursera, MasterClass, Udacity, a DataCamp hiring PDF, two DataCamp ads as copied on Built In, Per Scholas, and Woven by Toyota. Meta's job URLs returned errors. Netflix was not retrieved. Duolingo's own apply URL did not render the job.

## The index is a list, and the essay lives somewhere else

Two index shapes are both in production, and neither one uses the row to summarize the job. **Stripe's public list is `https://stripe.com/careers/search`, not `https://stripe.com/jobs`.** The jobs URL is the essay: a mission line, craft sections, a stats strip, and an FAQ on fit, growth, the hiring process, and recruiter fraud. The search URL opens with a one-line headline, "Assess your fit," and filters for company, team, office, and role type, then rows of title, team, and one location. The same listing is repeated once per office. Under the list sits an equal-opportunity statement and an accommodations address, not a second essay ([Stripe jobs](https://stripe.com/jobs); [Stripe open roles](https://stripe.com/careers/search)). **Anthropic does the same split.** `https://www.anthropic.com/careers` holds principles, "How we support you," "How we hire," an AI-in-applications policy, and an FAQ, and it says "competitive compensation" without a range. `https://www.anthropic.com/careers/jobs` is the list: team groups with open-role counts, columns for team, role, and location, and an Apply link that leaves the site for Greenhouse. The fetched index was cut off after the early groups, so the later departments were not read ([Anthropic careers](https://www.anthropic.com/careers); [Anthropic jobs](https://www.anthropic.com/careers/jobs)).

Notion and Linear make the essay the page and park the list underneath it. Notion's `https://www.notion.com/careers` runs a video, four named values, life-at-Notion city blocks, a benefits catalog, and only then "Open Positions," filterable by location and department and grouped under department headings. A row is one link whose text is the title plus the locations ([Notion careers](https://www.notion.com/careers)). Linear's `https://linear.app/careers` runs a manifesto, a video, talks, and eleven numbered working principles before "Open roles," grouped by team anchors rather than a filter bar. A row is title, a region such as "North America," and "Learn more" ([Linear careers](https://linear.app/careers)). Vercel's fetched index at `https://vercel.com/careers` is closer to the short shape: a headline and a flat list of title, sometimes a location, and "Read more" / "Learn more." The visible text showed no filter labels, but the HTML contains the words Filter, Department, Location, and Search, so a client-only filter is not ruled out ([Vercel careers](https://vercel.com/careers)).

Big-tech catalogs are search products. Google's opened job URL is the results page with one job selected: **"3,442 jobs matched,"** twenty cards of title and location, then the posting ([Google Staff Software Engineer](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/109329274653024966-staff-software-engineer-data-processing-platform)). Amazon's search is a sortable card list with category filters; a card shows title, locations, job id, a posted date, and a basic-qualifications preview ([Amazon software-engineer search](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/search?base_query=software+engineer&category%5B%5D=software-development&country%5B%5D=USA)). Microsoft's job page is fed by an apply API, and the index configuration includes remote, distance, work site, seniority, profession, discipline, role type, and employment type ([Microsoft Software Engineer](https://apply.careers.microsoft.com/careers/job/1970393557037774)). Across the product indexes, **the row holds title, place, and sometimes team. It does not hold responsibilities, a long about blurb, or an interview outline.** Anthropic is the exception that prints "Apply" on the row and sends the click straight to Greenhouse. Stripe, Linear, and Vercel keep the candidate on a first-party URL. Notion's rows go to `jobs.ashbyhq.com`.

Education boards that were actually open are short catalogs, not instructor marketplaces. Coursera's Greenhouse board, titled as Coursera and Udemy, listed **16 jobs and no instructor, curriculum, or learning-designer title** ([Coursera board](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/coursera)). MasterClass listed **six jobs and no learning-designer title**; an older learning-experience URL redirected to that board ([MasterClass jobs](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/masterclass)). Khan Academy listed **six jobs**, of which the two content seats opened in full were fixed-term assessment and bilingual activity roles, not a general instructor seat ([Khan Academy jobs](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy)). Udacity's mentor page puts a short argument and four hiring steps above a list of contractor openings ([Udacity mentor page](https://www.udacity.com/become-a-mentor)). The pattern a small company can copy is the row, not the headcount: title, an optional true constraint that is not a city, and a quiet link. The essay, if any, sits above the list or on a parent URL, and it stays off the row.

## The job page starts with the title and an Apply control

Every job page that rendered opens on the title and puts Apply within reach of that title, then repeats the action as a form or a second button after the prose. Stripe's AI Engineer page, posted in the page data on 7 October 2026, uses a named sidebar of four facts (company, team, office locations, employment type) and a sticky Apply. The buttons link to a separate apply URL, and the labels repeat: "Apply now" and "Apply for this role," including a control after the closing heading ([Stripe AI Engineer](https://stripe.com/careers/listing/ai-engineer/8144196)). Linear's Product Engineer page is one column: a breadcrumb, the title, the line "Full-time (North America)," an Apply link to `#apply`, the description, then an embedded Ashby form. No sidebar class was in that HTML ([Linear Product Engineer](https://linear.app/careers/0c7c2e26-0a98-42cf-a47c-9a3999fb513b)). Vercel prints department, "United States," and "Full Time" above the title, then the body, then "Apply Now," and no sidebar class was found ([Vercel Software Engineer, Backend](https://vercel.com/careers/software-engineer-backend-us-5430088004)). Anthropic's opened Greenhouse post puts "Back to jobs," the title, a three-city location line, and an Apply button above the description, then "Apply for this job" and the form. Classes include a pay-ranges hook and tags; **no sidebar class was in the class list** ([Anthropic Greenhouse job](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5026097008)).

Google, Microsoft, and Amazon confirm that the facts which survive a long description are the ones outside the prose. On the opened Google posting, company, "San Francisco, CA, USA," the level "Advanced," share, and Apply sit before the qualifications. There is no posted date, no job-id label, and no employment type on the page ([Google Staff Software Engineer](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/109329274653024966-staff-software-engineer-data-processing-platform)). Microsoft's reading order is one column: title, "United States, Washington, Redmond," "Apply now," then **eight labeled facts** (job number 200061216, date posted 9 October 2026, work site, travel, profession, discipline, role type, and employment type "Full-Time"), then the description. Company and benefits are a second tab, not a second column of facts ([Microsoft Software Engineer](https://apply.careers.microsoft.com/careers/job/1970393557037774); [Microsoft job JSON](https://apply.careers.microsoft.com/api/apply/v2/jobs/1970393557037774?domain=microsoft.com)). Amazon is the clearest two-column board: from the medium breakpoint, title and job id sit in a wide column and "Apply now" aligns to the end; the body is Description plus qualifications; a sidebar holds location, job category, a related-jobs mount whose heading was not in the static HTML, and share links. Employment type is not a visible field on that posting, and the detail HTML has no posted date. A search-card date for the same id conflicts with the page and is not a detail-page field ([Amazon job 10575919](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/10575919/software-development-engineer-amazon-fulfillment-technologies-aft-platform-engineering-services)). Meta's official URLs for the sampled requisition returned an error or an empty title, so Apply placement and column count there are unknown ([Meta Careers job URL](https://www.metacareers.com/jobs/1187958502273564)).

Notion's candidate chrome is the gap in this set. The Ashby URL returned only the document title to the fetcher. The heading order below comes from `descriptionHtml` on Ashby's posting API for the same id, which also showed department, team, a primary location, and employment type FullTime, and **no compensation field** ([Ashby posting API for Notion](https://api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/notion); [Notion Ashby job](https://jobs.ashbyhq.com/notion/def3f337-5593-491c-b34d-e0b53f2a5cac)). Ashby's own docs, not that render, say the candidate rail is title, location, employment type, location type, department, an optional deadline, compensation only if display is on, then the Overview, then the Application tab ([Ashby job postings](https://docs.ashbyhq.com/job-postings)). The layout lesson is narrow. **A sidebar is optional. Apply next to the title is not.** A company with no offices, no job number, and no employment type should copy Linear, Vercel, and Anthropic's single column, not Stripe's four-fact rail or Microsoft's eight labels. Copying the rail without the facts is how a city and a contract type get invented.

## Work and the bar fill the middle; the interview does not

The systems do not ship the essay outline. Greenhouse, Ashby, and Lever store structured facts (title, location, team or department, a workplace type, and sometimes pay) and leave the headings to the employer. Greenhouse's June 2026 help article asks for a title, a short blurb plus responsibility bullets, a hard-skill list, and a closing company block that can hold benefits and an equal-opportunity statement. Its 2019 public template names the fill-in headings **"Who will love this job," "What you'll do," "You should have," and "Pay, perks and such,"** and it tells the writer to keep a single call to action: finish the application. It does not include an interview heading or a how-to-apply heading ([Greenhouse writing help](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360003565931-Best-practices-Write-an-effective-job-description); [Greenhouse template](https://www.greenhouse.com/guidance/bookmark-this-template-for-job-descriptions)). Ashby gives one Description field. Lever's editor is an Opening, repeatable Sections, an optional salary block, and a Closing. The official Lever help pages did not render in this pass; those box names come from the help index plus one older article, and the live Woven post confirms the shape ([Lever posting templates](https://help.lever.co/s/article/Creating-and-using-job-posting-templates); [Woven by Toyota](https://jobs.lever.co/woven-by-toyota/d569edcd-2f38-432d-92d8-ca32c1f74865)).

On the opened product jobs, the middle is three blocks, and the names differ. Stripe runs Who We Are, About Stripe, About The Team, What You'll Do, Responsibilities, Minimum Requirements, Preferred Qualifications, Who You Are, in-office expectations, then Pay and benefits. Notion's stored HTML runs Who We Are, About the Role, What You'll Achieve, Skills You'll Need to Bring, Nice to Haves, A Note on AI, and Equal Opportunity. Linear uses lead-ins rather than a clean H2 stack: location and work mode, what you'll do, what we're looking for, the stack, what we offer. Vercel runs About Vercel, About the Role, What You Will Do, About You, Benefits and Compensation, Disclosures. Anthropic runs About Anthropic, about the role, responsibilities, a fit list, a stronger-candidate list, a "candidates need not have" list, logistics, how the company is different, and a closing invitation. **None of those five job pages has a heading for the interview process.** Stripe's hiring FAQ and Anthropic's "How we hire" live on the brand URL. Linear only links out for the work-trial story ([Stripe AI Engineer](https://stripe.com/careers/listing/ai-engineer/8144196); [Linear Product Engineer](https://linear.app/careers/0c7c2e26-0a98-42cf-a47c-9a3999fb513b); [Anthropic careers](https://www.anthropic.com/careers)).

Big tech does not share one outline, and compensation is never its own heading on the four companies checked. Google puts **Minimum qualifications and Preferred qualifications before About the job, and Responsibilities after the pay paragraph.** Microsoft and Amazon put the narrative first (Overview or Description), then qualifications, and fold the work into that narrative at Amazon rather than under a Responsibilities heading. Equal opportunity is a closing paragraph at Google, Microsoft, and Amazon, not a mid-page heading. A numeric range is present on the Google, Microsoft, and Amazon pages that were opened; the figures are omitted here on purpose ([Google Staff Software Engineer](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/109329274653024966-staff-software-engineer-data-processing-platform); [Microsoft Software Engineer](https://apply.careers.microsoft.com/careers/job/1970393557037774); [Amazon job 10575919](https://www.amazon.jobs/en/jobs/10575919/software-development-engineer-amazon-fulfillment-technologies-aft-platform-engineering-services)). Google's qualifications-first order is the outlier. The education posts and Greenhouse's own template put the company or the role, then the work, then the bar. That is the order to follow when the decision is about artifacts rather than a leveled engineering ladder.

Education ads that were open use that same skeleton and get specific by naming the learner and the artifact, then stopping. Khan Academy's two opened roles share one stack: a scam notice, about the company, the role, responsibilities or "What You'll Do," what you bring, perks, a pay-band paragraph, more about the company, seven values, and equal employment. The Kids role is bilingual English and Spanish activities and assessments for ages 2 to 8, tied to named early-learning standards, with editorial review and classroom tries. The ELA role is assessment items from blueprints for grades 3–5. **Neither ad names a lesson count, a sample lesson, or interview rounds.** Both are 24-month terms, and both warn that recruiters use only the company's own domain and will not ask for money ([Khan ELA assessment](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8224828); [Khan Kids bilingual](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8204881)). The live Per Scholas curriculum-leadership post on Greenhouse is the cleanest education-shaped example with no salary figure: about the organization, a fact block, who the role is for, what you'll do (roadmap, outlines, objectives, assessments, instructor and learner materials, launch handoff, revision from completion and placement data), professional then preferred qualifications, benefits, a questions line, equal employment, then the form ([Per Scholas curriculum role](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/perscholashires/jobs/4716529006)).

Where a loop is published, it belongs to people who originate a course, and it asks for work rather than narrating a theater schedule. DataCamp's instructor PDF, which has no date on the page and is dated 28 November 2022 by a search index, so it is not a confirmed October 2026 staff process, lists review, an outline that can be sent back, a **3–5 minute screencast** of one lesson, a committee, an offer conversation, and a contract, over about four to six weeks ([DataCamp instructor PDF](https://cdn.datacamp.com/DC%20Instructor%20Hiring%20Process.pdf)). The instructor marketing page itself was blocked by a bot check and was not fully opened. Udacity's mentor page publishes application, an online assessment, interviews, and training by enrollment; the two contractor ads that were opened do not repeat those steps. They say people join a pool and hear the project later ([Udacity mentor page](https://www.udacity.com/become-a-mentor); [Udacity Content Maintenance Specialist](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/udacity/jobs/8694065002)). Google's opened YouTube learning post is sales-enablement curriculum for Ads sellers, with the same qualifications-then-responsibilities shape as the other Google job, not a public-instructor template ([Google YouTube learning role](https://www.google.com/about/careers/applications/jobs/results/102246871434437318-principal-lead-global-product-learning-experiences-youtube)). Staff ads at Khan, MasterClass, and that Google post stop at Apply. A small company should do the same unless it will actually read an outline or a short sample.

## Pay, a city, and a contract label can stay off the page

No opened posting uses the phrase "to be discussed" for pay or for a city. The real pattern is omission or a non-numeric sentence, with the rest of the page intact. Greenhouse's pay-transparency field can be set to **N/A** when no rule applies, and the help text says to keep a typed range out of the description when a rule does apply ([Greenhouse pay transparency](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/10028084062491)). Ashby shows compensation only when display is turned on, and a range is required only in that case; the docs also describe keeping the number off the board and sending it by email ([Ashby compensation](https://docs.ashbyhq.com/compensation)). Lever's older help text says the salary section appears only when minimum and maximum are filled ([Lever salary behavior, 2024](https://lever-old.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042220512-Creating-and-managing-job-postings)). The live proof is the Woven by Toyota post: the body still runs about the company, role overview, key responsibilities, required qualifications, preferred qualifications, success measures, a short note on how interviews are held, benefits, and equal opportunity, while the offer line says competitive and based on experience and **no numeric range is on the page** ([Woven by Toyota](https://jobs.lever.co/woven-by-toyota/d569edcd-2f38-432d-92d8-ca32c1f74865)). Linear's fetched job says "Competitive salary and equity" and does not show a range. Per Scholas prints no figure at all. Both opened Udacity contractor ads say compensation varies and then state no number. Notion's API record for the sampled job has no compensation object. DataCamp's curriculum-manager text, as opened on Built In, had no salary heading. Those are the models. Stripe, Vercel, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Khan Academy, MasterClass, and the Duolingo reprint on LINGUIST List do show a range. The digits are not repeated here, because copying them would describe those employers.

Location is the field the systems refuse to leave blank, and a city is still not required. Greenhouse says **job-post location is required** and, by default, free text, so a country or the word Remote can fill it ([Greenhouse locations](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360027858591-Configure-structured-job-post-locations)). Per Scholas shows "United States" in the chrome and "Remote" inside the prose, not a city. Vercel shows "United States." Linear shows "North America." A hand-built page that is not sitting on Greenhouse can omit the line. What was not observed is a blank, a "city to be discussed," or a duty to name a street. Inventing Toronto, or San Francisco, or Pittsburgh, because Stripe, Notion, and the Duolingo notice name cities, is the failure mode. Khan Academy does the opposite of postponing place: the opened ads limit applicants to the continental United States, Hawaii, and Canada, on site in San Mateo or remote inside that set. That is a rule the company has. A company without a rule should not borrow it.

Employment type is the easiest label to skip and the one most often copied by reflex. Greenhouse documents it as **a custom job field enabled by request, not a default on every job** ([Greenhouse custom job fields](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/202609855-Add-a-custom-job-field)). That help page was read from the search index, not re-fetched in full, and the live boards agree with the caution: Google's opened posting and Amazon's opened posting do not show full-time versus contract, while Stripe, Linear, Vercel, and Microsoft do, because those roles have a type. MasterClass's board happens to label one seat CONTRACT and another Temporary. Udacity's opened ads are contractor pools that still avoid printing a number and postpone which course and which week. Ashby will print an employment type if the job settings contain one. **If the company has not decided, the honest page leaves the label off.** Writing "full-time" or "contract" to look complete is an invention, the same class of invention as a salary figure.

The sections that become false when copied are the support and scale blocks, not the work. Khan's perks include time off as needed, eight named wellness days in 2026, parental leave described only as generous, a retirement match of 4 percent, and medical, dental, vision, and life insurance, beside a claim of more than 181 million registered learners ([Khan Kids bilingual](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/khanacademy/jobs/8204881)). DataCamp's architect ad, on Built In, lists a retirement match, insurance, equity, retreats, a learning budget, and an equipment budget beside a claim of over 20 million learners. Built In's own summary and a "Junior" stamp on that ad are not DataCamp headings ([DataCamp architect on Built In](https://builtin.com/job/learning-solution-architect-ai-software-engineering/11543711)). Anthropic's brand page and Linear's "What we offer" describe health coverage, leave, and equity that belong to those companies. Udacity's contractor ads do not list health insurance; what would still be false to paste is the Accenture legal machinery around them. A scam warning is portable only as an idea. Khan's notice is tied to `@khanacademy.org`. The sentence that survives is "we will not ask applicants for money," rewritten for the company that means it.

| Section on the opened pages | What the pages actually do | Usable with no invented salary, city, or contract type |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | Universal | Yes |
| Apply, then the form | On every rendered job; Meta unseen | Yes. A link or a form, not an essay called "How to apply" |
| Who the learner is, and the role | Untitled blurb or "About" / "The role" | Yes, if it names the learner and the assignment and skips headcount |
| The work | Responsibilities, or Amazon's Description | Yes. Artifacts: lessons, scripts, assessments, instructor materials, revision from feedback |
| The bar | One list, or required then preferred | Yes. Greenhouse's template keeps both inside "You should have" |
| Interview loop | Absent on the five product jobs and on the opened Khan, MasterClass, and Google staff ads | Only a sample the company will read. Otherwise omit |
| Numeric pay | Present at Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Stripe, Vercel, Anthropic, Khan, MasterClass | No |
| Pay with no number | Linear, Woven, Udacity; absent at Notion and Per Scholas | Yes. Omit the section, or one non-numeric sentence |
| Benefits catalog | Brand pages and the larger education ads | Only items the company provides. Otherwise omit |
| City or office list | Common, and required as some location string on Greenhouse | A true country or Remote, or omit on a hand-built page. Never a borrowed city |
| Full-time versus contract | Present when the employer has decided; not a Greenhouse default | Omit until it is true |
| Equal opportunity | A closer almost everywhere; a heading mainly in weaker Meta extracts | A short statement in the company's own words |

## Conclusion

The page is complete without the three facts that are tempting to invent. It runs in this order: **the title; Apply beside that title; who the learner is, in one short block with no borrowed reach or headcount; the role, stated as the assignment and only those constraints that are already true, with no city and no full-time or contract label; what the person will make, as concrete artifacts and the review of those artifacts; what the person needs to bring, required first and preferred second, or one list in that same order; a sample or a later project assignment only when that is how hiring actually works; pay and support with no figure, or nothing in that slot; an equal-opportunity close, plus a line that applicants will not be asked for money; and the application form.** That order is Greenhouse's template plus the live education posts, not Google's qualifications-first ladder and not Microsoft's fact rail. A required location box on Greenhouse is filled with a true country or with Remote, and it is not given a section of its own unless that word is true.

The jobs index is a separate, dull list, not a second essay and not a stack of miniature job ads. Each row is the title, optionally one true constraint that is not a salary, a city, or an employment type, and a quiet link. Rows can sit under a handful of real groupings such as the kind of work. Filters are worth adding only when the list is long, as on Stripe's search page and Anthropic's jobs page. Responsibilities, pay, interview steps, and benefits stay off the row. Any mission, any support the company actually provides, and any hiring steps that are real live above the list or on a parent URL, the way Stripe's FAQ lives on `https://stripe.com/jobs` and Anthropic's "How we hire" lives on `https://www.anthropic.com/careers`.
